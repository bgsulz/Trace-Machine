Some watermarks can be read without asking the company that made them. Trace Machine checks for these on every image, on its own server.

Adobe TrustMark

TrustMark is the open watermark behind Adobe's Durable Content Credentials. It hides a short identifier in the pixels that survives screenshots, re-compression, and metadata stripping. The identifier links the image back to its C2PA manifest in a Content Credentials database, so finding one means the image was registered for Content Credentials even if the manifest has since been stripped from the file.

A TrustMark marks provenance, not AI generation: cameras, editors, and AI tools can all apply one. Because a decoder can occasionally "find" a mark in an ordinary photo by chance, Trace Machine only reports a mark when it also reads the same identifier from slightly edited copies of the image.

Important limitation: These checks are positive-only. A hit is useful evidence, but a miss does not mean the image is clean: most invisible watermarks (including Google's SynthID and Meta's Content Seal) can only be read by their makers, and heavy edits can erase any of them.

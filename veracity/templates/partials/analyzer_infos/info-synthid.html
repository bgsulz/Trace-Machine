Some AI providers hide an imperceptible watermark in the pixels of every image they generate. Unlike metadata, these watermarks survive screenshots, resizing, and re-encoding reasonably well, but only the provider can read them, through its own public checker. Trace Machine links you to those checkers ("portals") and records what people report back.

The portals

  • Google (SynthID): Google embeds its SynthID watermark in images from Gemini and its Nano Banana models. To check an image, sign in to Gemini, upload it, and ask whether it was made with Google AI.
  • OpenAI (Verify): OpenAI licensed SynthID from Google and now embeds it, alongside a C2PA manifest, in images from ChatGPT Images and its API. OpenAI Verify checks for both. Use it for suspected OpenAI images; Google says Gemini only recognizes content made by Google's own tools. Where it's enabled, Trace Machine can also run this check for you with one click through OpenAI's API. Those results are labeled as automated checks and count for more than community reports.
  • Meta (Identify): Meta uses its own imperceptible watermark, Content Seal, for images from Meta AI. It isn't compatible with SynthID or C2PA, so it has its own checker, Meta Identify.
Important limitation: Each portal only recognizes its own provider's watermark. A negative result rules out that provider, not AI in general, and many generators don't watermark at all. Portal reports on Trace Machine are community evidence: a negative report means someone checked and got no positive result.

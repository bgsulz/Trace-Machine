Some AI providers hide an imperceptible watermark in the pixels of every image they generate. Unlike metadata, these watermarks survive screenshots, resizing, and re-encoding reasonably well, but only the provider can read them, through its own public checker. Trace Machine links you to those checkers ("portals") and records what people report back.

The checks, in order

  1. Automated check (OpenAI): OpenAI licensed SynthID from Google and embeds it, alongside a C2PA manifest, in images from ChatGPT Images and its API. Where it's enabled, Trace Machine can check for both with one click through OpenAI's API. Those results are labeled as automated checks and count for more than community reports. It only finds OpenAI images, so if it finds nothing or fails, move on.
  2. Google SynthID Detector: Google embeds SynthID in images from Gemini and its Nano Banana models, and partners like OpenAI embed it too. Google's official SynthID Detector reads all of them and says which provider made the image, so you report "Says Google" or "Says OpenAI". You'll need to sign in with a Google, Apple, or ChatGPT account.
  3. Meta Identify: Meta uses its own imperceptible watermark, Content Seal, for images from Meta AI. It isn't compatible with SynthID or C2PA, so it has its own checker.

If you can't use the SynthID Detector (it's rate-limited, or you'd rather not sign in), OpenAI Verify also checks OpenAI images for both Content Credentials and SynthID.

Important limitation: Each portal only recognizes the watermarks it was built for. A negative result rules out those watermarks, not AI in general, and many generators don't watermark at all. Portal reports on Trace Machine are community evidence: a negative report means someone checked and got no positive result.
